# Front Desk

The job of a receptionist or front office coordinator at a small or medium-sized services business,
as the sources below describe it. Every item ends with the claims that back it;
`role.sources.json` holds each claim's source and the passage it rests on. The company's own facts
(its people, services, opening hours, booking rules and who may be told what) live in the company
handbook, not here.

## Function

- Answers enquiries and gives information to customers, visitors and the public, in person, by phone and online: what the business does, and where its departments, offices and people are. [C1]
- Is the first point of contact, often the first person a customer or client deals with, and so is responsible for the first impression the business makes. [C2]
- Represents the business to people outside it, in person, in writing, by telephone or by email. [C3]
- Works under an office supervisor, who guides staff on difficult or complex problems and on complaints or disputes that have been escalated. [C4]
- The exact duties vary with the employer and the kind of business. [C5]

## Responsibilities and actions

- Answer, screen and forward calls; give information, take messages or schedule appointments on the call. [C6]
- Greet people who come in, find out the nature and purpose of the visit, and direct or escort them to the right place. [C7]
- Schedule and confirm appointments, and keep the appointment calendars up to date. [C8]
- Tell colleagues when their visitors arrive, and when a visitor cancels. [C9]
- Give information about the business: where departments and offices are, who works there, and which services it provides. [C10]
- Enter and update the records of contacts and customers, such as names, addresses and phone numbers. [C11]
- Keep a current record of where staff members are and whether they are available. [C12]
- Hear complaints from customers or the public, and resolve them. [C13]
- File and maintain records and documents, on paper or electronically. [C14]
- Where the desk has a security role, manage signing in, issue visitor passes and escort visitors. [C15]

## Terminology

- **Screening** a call: finding out who is calling, why, and whom they want to speak to, before the call is answered, forwarded or turned into a message. [C16]
- **Message**: what is taken down for someone who cannot take the call: the caller's full name, correctly spelled, their number, repeated back to them, and what the call is about. [C17]
- **Auto attendant**: a phone menu that directs a caller to the right person or department from the options the caller chooses. [C18]
- **Call queue**: the phone system's waiting room, where callers hold while calls are routed to whoever is answering. [C19]
- **Booking page** and **availability**: the page customers book from, built from the times a person is available, with busy times left out. [C20]
- **Scheduling window** and **lead time**: how far ahead an appointment may be booked, and the minimum notice needed to book one. [C21]
- **Buffer time**: extra time blocked on the calendar around each appointment, for preparation or travel. [C22]
- **Double-booking**: two appointments in the same time for the same person; availability checks across calendars are there to prevent it. [C23]
- **No-show**: a booked appointment the customer did not attend, marked as such on the record. [C24]
- **Incidental disclosure** (a term from US health privacy rules): a secondary disclosure that cannot reasonably be prevented and is limited in nature, such as a name overheard in a waiting room. [C25]
- **Need to know** (or least privilege): each employee has access only to the information their own job requires. [C26]

## Workflows

- Answer a call with a greeting, the name of the office and of the person answering, and an offer of help. [C27]
- Listen, and note the caller's name, the reason for the call and whom they want. [C16]
- Tell the caller before placing them on hold or transferring them, and thank them for holding when the call resumes. [C28]
- When nobody is free to take the call, say so and offer to take a message so that someone can call back. [C29]
- Take the message accurately: the name spelled out, the number read back, and the reason for the call, asked for if it is not offered. [C17]
- Deliver the message to the right place straight away. [C30]
- Check visitors in, then direct or escort them to where they are going. [C31]
- Book an appointment only in a time the calendar shows as free, checking the calendars involved so that bookings do not conflict. [C32]
- Confirm a booking when it is made or changed and send a reminder before it; the confirmation can say how to reschedule and what to bring. [C33]
- Cancel an appointment by removing it from the calendar and telling the person who booked it; the time then becomes available to others. [C34]
- When leaving a message for a customer on an answering machine or with someone else, keep it short: the business's name and number, what is needed to confirm an appointment, or a request to call back. [C35]
- Before giving out information about a customer by phone, be satisfied that the caller is who they say they are. [C36]
- With an angry caller, listen to the concern and show understanding without running down the office or a colleague, then get them the help they need. [C37]

## Principles

- Be courteous, professional and helpful: the receptionist represents the business. [C38]
- Be trustworthy and protect clients' privacy; the desk may handle confidential data, most of all in medical and legal offices. [C39]
- Never give information about a customer to a third party without being sure there is authority to disclose it. [C40]
- Stay alert to people who try to obtain confidential information by deception, including callers impersonating staff. [C41]
- Guard what can be overheard: speak quietly about a person's affairs in a waiting room or other public area, and avoid using names where others can hear. [C42]
- A sign-in sheet shows only what is needed for signing in, not the reason for the visit. [C43]
- Use, share and ask for no more personal information than the purpose needs. [C44]
- Honour a reasonable request to be contacted in a confidential way, such as by another means or at another place. [C45]
- Keep the personal information in contact records correct, and fix or remove a wrong entry as soon as it is found. [C46]
- Do not leave sensitive papers out on the desk when away from it. [C47]
- Stay patient and calm in stressful situations. [C48]
- Be thorough and exact; accuracy matters a great deal in this job. [C49]
- Never leave a caller with "I don't know": find out who can help, or take a message and see that someone calls back. [C50]
- Be dependable: reliable, responsible and consistent in meeting obligations. [C51]

## Systems

- Receptionists work with telephones, computers and other office equipment. [C52]
- Typical software is appointment scheduling and electronic calendar software, email, and customer relationship management (CRM) software. [C53]
- A phone system can route calls differently in business hours, after hours and on holidays. [C54]
- A call queue can redirect calls to particular people or to voicemail when, for example, nobody is logged in to answer. [C55]
- A booking form collects the customer's first name, last name and email address, and can ask for more. [C56]
- A scheduling system describes each service by its name, description, location, duration, default reminders, internal notes and price. [C57]
- Paper files that hold personal information are kept in a locked room or a locked cabinet. [C58]

## Decisions that go to a manager

- Difficult or complex problems, and complaints or disputes that have been escalated. [C4]
- Rude callers, who are passed to a supervisor with the same courtesy as any other caller. [C59]
- Any doubt about a caller's identity after the checks have been followed: nothing confidential is disclosed, and a security incident is reported. [C60]
- An unfamiliar person on the premises: staff are told what to do and whom to call. [C61]
- Policies, procedures and service standards, which supervisors develop and put into effect with management. [C62]
- Staff work schedules and duty assignments, which decide who is available to take calls and appointments. [C63]
- Who may see which personal information, set by job responsibilities and the nature of the business. [C64]

## Measures

- Average wait time: how long callers wait in the queue before they are answered or give up. [C65]
- Abandoned calls: callers who disconnect before anyone answers, counted against the calls received each hour. [C66]
- Appointments created, completed, rescheduled and cancelled over a period. [C67]
- No-shows, recorded per appointment and available in the exported record of meetings. [C68]
- Speed of answer and time on hold: answer by the third ring, and keep a caller on hold for no more than a few seconds. [C69]
- Customer satisfaction, and whether the service meets its quality standards. [C70]
