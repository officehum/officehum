# Office Manager

The job of an office manager or administrative services coordinator at a small or medium-sized
business, as the sources below describe it, weighted toward the intake side of the job: reading what
comes in, deciding where it belongs and how soon it matters, and keeping the person who runs the
business informed. Every item ends with the claims that back it; `role.sources.json` holds each
claim's source and the passage it rests on. The company's own facts (its people, departments, ticket
types, urgency levels and policies) live in the company handbook, not here.

## Function

- Oversees the day-to-day operations of an office or department. [C1]
- Plans and coordinates the organisation's administrative services, such as records and information management, mail distribution and other office support. [C2]
- In a small organisation, one person may direct all of the support services, often under the title of business office manager. [C3]
- Is the point where incoming material and enquiries are read, their significance judged, and each one sent on to the person it is for. [C4]
- Keeps senior management informed through reports on how operations stand. [C5]
- Exists to help the organisation run efficiently. [C6]

## Responsibilities and actions

- Open, read, sort and route incoming mail, email and other material, and answer the routine items. [C7]
- Answer calls, give callers information, take messages, or pass the call to the right individual. [C8]
- Find the relevant files and attach them to incoming correspondence that needs a reply, so whoever answers has what they need. [C9]
- Coordinate the flow of information inside the organisation and with other organisations. [C10]
- Act as the liaison for requests and complaints that concern the office. [C11]
- Coordinate work with other departments and supervisors, such as finance and human resources. [C12]
- Prepare and issue work schedules, deadlines and duty assignments for office staff. [C13]
- Research, compile and prepare the reports, correspondence and other information management asks for. [C14]
- Watch stock levels, and order, store and hand out supplies. [C15]
- Deal with outside vendors and service providers so that the office gets what it needs from them. [C16]
- Arrange office services such as equipment, maintenance and repairs, relocation and security. [C17]
- Manage the paper and electronic filing systems: record information, update paperwork and maintain documents. [C18]
- Keep records of inventory, orders, supplies and equipment maintenance. [C19]
- Explain work procedures and company policies to staff. [C20]
- Look at how the office's processes work and recommend changes to procedure or policy that would improve them. [C21]

## Terminology

- **Triage**: the review of incoming items, where each is checked, updated and prioritised before it joins a team's workflow. [C22]
- **Routing**: directing an incoming item to whoever should handle it, in two stages: classification, then assignment. [C23]
- **Impact**: a measure of the effect of an issue, often judged by how many people or how much of the service it affects. [C24]
- **Urgency**: a measure of how long it will be until an issue has a significant effect on the business. [C25]
- **Priority**: the relative importance of an item, set from impact and urgency together, and used to decide how quickly it must be acted on. [C26]
- **Ticket status**: where an item stands in its pipeline; a typical default runs New, Waiting on contact, Waiting on us, Closed. [C27]
- **Resolved**: closed out as complete, as needing no further action, or as a duplicate of another item. [C28]
- **Records management**: the systematic control of how records are created, maintained, used and disposed of. [C29]
- **Records schedule** (or **retention policy**): the rule for how long each kind of information is kept before it is disposed of. [C30]
- **Shared mailbox**: one mailbox that several people can open, used for addresses such as company information, support or the front desk. [C31]
- **Facility management**: the function that brings together people, place and process in the built environment, a neighbouring part of the wider job. [C32]
- **Phishing**: a message built to trick staff into clicking a harmful link, opening a fake email or downloading a malicious attachment. [C33]

## Workflows

- Read each incoming item in full to judge its significance and plan who should receive it. [C4]
- Classify before assigning: first add what is known about the item, then use that to find the person best suited to it. [C23, C34]
- Decide on each new item: accept it into the workflow, decline it, mark it as a duplicate, or set it aside until a chosen time. [C35]
- When an item lacks the details needed to act on it, ask the person who raised it and hold the item in triage until they answer; a full request is what lets the right priority be set. [C36]
- Give every item a priority, since the priority decides how quickly it will be dealt with. [C26, C37]
- Assign responsibility for each item to a named person, who is told that it is now theirs. [C38]
- Order the work inside a queue so that time goes to the most important items first. [C39]
- Bring a parked item back when its time comes or when something new happens on it, whichever is first. [C40]
- Mark each item's outcome when it is finished: complete, no action needed, or duplicate. [C28]
- Write a regular update for the people who need it in three parts: what was accomplished, what needs input (blockers and open questions), and what comes next. [C41]
- Open the update with a tight headline and a few sentences that tell a busy reader what is happening, why it matters and what is needed from them. [C42]
- Check an unexpected or unusual request through a contact method already on file or a second channel, never by replying to the message or using the details inside it. [C43]
- File records regularly and carefully so that information can be found when it is needed. [C44]
- Define how long each kind of record is kept, in a policy of standard retention periods where possible; dispose of it when it is no longer needed, and be able to explain why it is no longer held. [C45, C72]

## Principles

- Judge urgency by how soon the business is affected, not by how a request is worded: urgent language is itself a warning sign of a fraudulent message. [C25, C46]
- Keep impact and urgency apart: an issue with a large impact can still have low urgency if its effect is a long way off. [C47]
- Treat a message that appears to come from a known contact with the same care, because the account may have been compromised and familiar ways of working can be imitated to obtain information or payments. [C48]
- Know how key tasks, such as payments, are normally done, so that an unusual request stands out. [C49]
- Record decisions and the actions taken, and document the work the office is responsible for. [C50]
- Be transparent in updates: say where the work is blocked, what has been tried, and what specific help is needed. [C51]
- Keep updates as short, clear and direct as possible, so that others can understand them quickly and act. [C52]
- A written update is a clear, accountable record of progress. [C53]
- Be thorough and pay attention to detail. [C54]
- Be dependable: reliable, responsible and consistent in meeting obligations. [C55]
- Stay patient and calm in stressful situations. [C56]
- Do not keep personal data for longer than it is needed. [C57]

## Systems

- A shared mailbox for the office's public addresses, from which permitted users can send as, or on behalf of, the mailbox. [C31, C58]
- A collaborative inbox, where conversations can be assigned to a member and searched by assignment status. [C38, C59]
- A ticketing or issue tracker with a triage inbox, rules that set an item's team, status, assignee, label and priority, and a named person responsible for incoming items. [C22, C60, C61]
- Ticket fields that carry the priority (the level of attention needed) and the source (how the item was created). [C62]
- Records tools such as file plans, indexes and access and security procedures. [C63]
- Computers for databases, word processing and email. [C10, C64]
- Office equipment such as copiers and phone systems, with repairs arranged when something fails. [C65]

## Decisions that go to a manager

- Difficult or complex problems, and complaints or disputes that have been escalated. [C66]
- Staffing decisions and changes to procedure: the office manager recommends, and management decides. [C67]
- Company policies, procedures and service standards, which are put into effect together with management. [C68]
- Decisions about personnel, resources, space and equipment, which are made with the other department leaders. [C69]
- Budget planning: the office analyses its financial activity and provides input to the budget. [C70]
- A disagreement over the priority an item was given, which goes to the manager of the desk. [C71]

## Measures

- Time to first assignment: from when an item is created to when a person is first assigned to it. [C73]
- Time to close: from when an item is created to when it is closed. [C74]
- Whether items are handled within the target time set for their priority. [C75]
- The number of items still unresolved (active). [C76]
- Adherence to quality standards, deadlines and proper procedures across the office's work. [C77]
- The accuracy of operational reports and schedules. [C5]
- What a review of records and reports shows about the work done. [C78]
- Whether information can be found when it is needed, and whether the office can explain why a record is no longer held. [C44, C45]
