# The shape of a digest

## Order of the sections

1. Done
2. Waiting on a person
3. Blocked

Done goes first: it is what a reader skims to see the day moved. Waiting on a person comes before
Blocked, because those are the items the reader has to act on.

## The shape of a line

One line per item: the item's own link or reference, a dash, then one plain sentence.

    item-12 — the delivery cannot go out until the van is fixed

Say what the item is in the words it arrived in, and say where it stands. A line that needs two
sentences can have them; a line that needs a paragraph is not a digest line.

## A worked example

Items given:

- item-31 — done: the rota went out
- item-32 — waiting on a person: the new terms need a decision
- item-33 — blocked: waiting on a signature from outside

The digest:

    Done

    - item-31 — the rota went out.

    Waiting on a person

    - item-32 — the new terms need a decision from a person.

    Blocked

    - item-33 — cannot move until a signature arrives from outside.

## An item with no status

Ask. "item-34 came in with no status — is it done, waiting on someone, or blocked?" Never guess: the
digest is what a person reads to decide where to spend their day, so a made-up status costs more than a
question.

## A day with nothing to report

Say that: "Nothing came in today." An empty digest says there is nothing to do; an invented one sends a
person chasing work that does not exist.
