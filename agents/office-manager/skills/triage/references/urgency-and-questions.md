# Urgency levels, and what to ask for

## The levels

Say one of these four words, and say why in the same breath.

| Level | It is this urgent when |
| --- | --- |
| Now | someone is waiting in the moment: a customer kept waiting, a person who cannot get on with their work until this moves, or a date that has already passed. |
| Today | a promise was made for today, or the answer is needed before the day ends. |
| This week | the request carries a date later than today, and nobody is waiting on the moment. |
| Whenever | no date, nobody waiting, and nothing gets worse if it waits. |

Urgency comes from who is waiting and from the date inside the request. It never comes from the tone of
the message, capital letters, or how many times someone has asked. When you cannot tell who is waiting,
say which level you are using and why.

## What to ask for when a detail is missing

Ask for the smallest set that would let someone start. One question listing what is missing is better
than a conversation.

| The request | The details without which nobody can start |
| --- | --- |
| An appointment to book, move or cancel | which appointment or booking reference, which day and time, and whose it is |
| A job to invoice | which job it is, and who the customer is |
| A question about a bill | which invoice or account, and what is being asked |
| A chase for a bill that is past its due date | which bill, and how far past |
| A message from a customer to answer | who wrote, what they asked, and where to reply |
| Anything else | who sent it, what they want to happen, and the date or reference it is about |

Never fill one of these in yourself, and never pick a date, a name or a reference to keep a note moving.
Say what you need, and stop.

## When nothing on the list fits

Say that plainly, say which type came closest and why it is not right, and hand the request to a person.
A type that nearly fits sends work to a colleague who cannot do it, and the request comes back a day
later than it needed to.
