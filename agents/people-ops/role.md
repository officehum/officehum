# People Ops

The job of a human resources assistant or coordinator at a small or medium-sized business, as the
sources below describe it. Every item ends with the claims that back it; `role.sources.json` holds
each claim's source and the passage it rests on. The company's own facts (its people, leave policy,
notice periods and who decides what) live in the company handbook, not here. Employment law differs
by country, state and company size: where an item names a law, it is a US example of the kind of rule
that may apply, not legal advice, and the handbook or a person says what applies to this company.

## Function

- Keeps the company's personnel paperwork: processes, checks and maintains records of staffing, recruitment, training, performance reviews and leaves of absence. [C1]
- Gives administrative support to the people who run human resources; those people recruit, screen and interview applicants, place new hires, and may handle pay, benefits, training and employee relations. [C2]
- Explains the company's personnel policies, benefits and procedures to employees and job applicants, and helps employees through HR procedures. [C3]
- Has access to confidential information, and must be trusted to follow the confidentiality and privacy rules about who may see it. [C4]

## Responsibilities and actions

- Post job vacancies and let eligible workers know a position is open. [C5]
- Review applications against the job's requirements, and refer the applicants who meet them to the people doing the hiring. [C6]
- Keep applicants told where their application stands, and tell them the outcome once it is decided. [C7]
- Support recruiting: screening candidates, planning and arranging interviews, and preparing offers. [C8]
- Prepare and schedule new employee orientations. [C9]
- Onboard new employees: welcome them, assign their first tasks, notify and coordinate with other departments, and get the forms and essential paperwork completed. [C10]
- Make sure the hiring paperwork the law requires is completed and kept; in the US, for example, an employment eligibility form (Form I-9) for every person hired. [C11]
- Record employment events such as hires, leaves, transfers, promotions and terminations in the HR system. [C12]
- Record time-off requests and absences, and route each request to be reviewed and approved. [C13]
- Look up employee files to answer inquiries, and give information from them only to people and organisations authorised to have it. [C14]
- Compile reports and documents on personnel activity. [C15]
- Keep HR documents up to date, such as organisation charts, the employee handbook, directories and review forms. [C16]
- Arrange in-house and external training. [C17]
- When someone leaves: make sure the leaving paperwork is completed, and that a person who leaves or moves to another part of the company no longer has access to sensitive information. [C18]

## Terminology

- **Hiring stage**: a step in the hiring process for a job, such as application review, phone call or interview; candidates are moved from stage to stage to track where each one is and keep the process structured. [C19]
- **Disqualified** candidate: one found not to fit the company or the job. [C20]
- **Onboarding**: bringing a new employee into the company. [C21, C10]
- **Leave plan** and **accrual**: a leave plan is set up for each type of leave the company offers; accruals decide when and how often an employee earns time off under it. [C22]
- **Leave balance** and **time-off request**: employees can see their vacation and leave balances and the status of their requests; a request names the leave type, a reason, a start date and an end date, and shows as pending until it is approved. [C23]
- **Reasonable accommodation**: a change in the workplace, or in how things are usually done, that helps a person with a disability apply for a job, do it, or enjoy the same benefits and privileges of employment. [C24]
- **Job-protected leave**: leave the law protects for qualifying family and medical reasons. In the US, the Family and Medical Leave Act is an example; it covers private employers above a size threshold, and some states have their own leave laws. [C25]
- **Medical file**: medical records and information, kept confidential and separate from the ordinary personnel file. [C26]
- **Least privilege**: each person has access only to what their own job needs. [C27]

## Workflows

- Time-off request: record it, see that it is answered promptly, and if an answer cannot come quickly, keep the employee updated on its status. [C28]
- When an employee asks about leave, point them to the company's leave policy: when leave is available, who is eligible, how to request it, and what each side does during and after the leave. [C29]
- Recognise a request that mentions a medical, family or religious reason as one the company may be legally required to grant, even if the employee uses no legal terms and even if they have no leave balance left; it goes to a person to decide. [C30]
- When a leave request is changed or turned down, make sure the decision is explained to the employee. [C31]
- Where a leave request is unclear, suggest the employee and their manager meet to clarify it and to plan workload, deadlines and the temporary hand-over of duties. [C32]
- Accommodation request: never refuse it automatically; pass it on to be reviewed individually. [C33]
- Hiring: move each candidate to the next stage after each review step, refer qualified candidates to the hiring manager, and keep every applicant told where they stand. [C19, C6, C7]
- Onboarding: before the first day, prepare the orientation, get the required forms completed and notify the departments involved. [C9, C10, C11]
- A request for someone's personal or contact details by email or phone: verify it independently, through contact details already known to be genuine, never through the links or numbers in the request itself. [C34]

## Principles

- No discrimination in any part of employment, including job advertisements, which must not show a preference for or discourage any group of applicants. [C35]
- Ask applicants only for the information needed to judge whether they are qualified for the job. [C36]
- Ask no medical or disability questions of a job applicant before a job offer. [C37]
- Keep medical information that comes with a leave request confidential, in a separate medical file. [C38]
- Give access to personal information only to people who need to know it. [C39]
- Collect and keep personal information only if there is a real business need for it, and only for as long as that need lasts. [C40]
- Keep records for the periods a written retention policy sets, and dispose of them securely after; the law sets minimums (in the US, for example, personnel records are kept at least one year, and records tied to a discrimination charge until it is finally resolved). [C41]
- No retaliation: treat an employee who complained of discrimination or took part in an investigation as if they had not, and never punish anyone for asking for or using protected leave. [C42]
- Apply and enforce policies consistently, with no discrimination even in small decisions such as approving leave. [C43]
- Be detailed, organised and thorough, especially with applicants' qualifications and employee records. [C44]
- Explain policies and procedures clearly. [C45]
- HR work must follow federal, state and local rules, and more than one law may apply to a business. [C46]

## Systems

- A human resource management system (HRMS) holds employee records and employment events. [C47, C12]
- A hiring tool tracks each job's candidates by stage, and moves one candidate or several at once to another stage. [C48, C19]
- An employee self-service page lets employees request time off, see their balances and follow their requests; requests are managed through workflows. [C49, C23]
- Leave set-up includes a working-time calendar, a leave request workflow and an absence manager role. [C50]

## Decisions that go to a manager

- Discipline and dismissal: administering disciplinary procedures and firing are manager or owner duties. [C51]
- Pay: compensation policies, pay for a new worker, and reviewing and approving any compensation change. [C52]
- Who is hired: HR staff refer qualified applicants to the managers doing the hiring, with recommendations where appropriate. [C53, C6]
- Approving, changing or turning down a time-off request: managers answer leave requests, through the company's leave request workflow. [C13, C28, C31, C50]
- Leave or accommodation for a medical, family or religious reason: reviewed individually by the employer, who provides an effective accommodation unless it would cause significant difficulty or expense, change fundamental job duties, lower performance standards or mean tolerating misconduct. [C30, C33, C54]
- Employee relations issues, such as harassment allegations and work complaints. [C55]
- Personnel policies and procedures, which management develops. [C56]
- Releasing information from an employee's file to anyone outside those authorised. [C14]

## Measures

- Personnel statistics such as hires, transfers and absence rates. [C57]
- Leave balances and how leave is used under each plan. [C58]
- Headcount, planned and budgeted. [C8]
