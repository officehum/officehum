# Sales / Account Manager

The job of a sales representative or account manager at a small or medium-sized services business,
as the sources below describe it. Every item ends with the claims that back it; `role.sources.json`
holds each claim's source and the passage it rests on. The company's own facts (its people, prices
and rules) live in the company handbook, not here.

## Function

- Sells the company's services to businesses or individuals, explaining the options and helping clients with their problems. [C1]
- Speaks for the company to people outside it, above all to customers and prospective customers. [C2]
- Builds working relationships with clients and keeps them up over time; the relationship starts, rather than ends, when a sale closes. [C3]
- Works under a sales manager, who directs the selling by setting territories and goals and by training the representatives. [C4]

## Responsibilities and actions

- Find prospective customers: from directories, from referrals by existing clients, and from events such as conferences and trade shows. [C5]
- Contact prospective and existing customers to talk through how the company's services could meet their needs. [C6]
- Answer customers' questions about the services, their prices and their availability, and about credit terms. [C7]
- Prepare proposals or presentations that set out what a service covers. [C8]
- Work out and compare the cost of services, and quote prices and terms: credit terms, contract terms and the dates the work will be done. [C9]
- Negotiate prices and the terms of sales or service agreements. [C10]
- Prepare the agreement or contract that completes a sale, and pass the order on to be fulfilled. [C11]
- Keep customer records in the company's systems. [C12]
- Stay in touch after the sale or the signature, to sort out problems and support the client. [C13]
- Keep an eye on the market and on competitors' services and prices. [C14]
- Bring in a colleague with the technical knowledge when a sale needs expertise the representative lacks, and share information on selling with colleagues. [C15]

## Terminology

- **Lead**: a potential buyer who has not yet been qualified. [C16]
- **Prospect** (or **qualified lead**): a lead found to be ready, willing and able to buy. **Qualifying** is finding out whether a lead has both the desire and the ability to buy. [C17]
- **Prospecting** is identifying potential buyers; the **preapproach** is the preparation and research done before a sales call. [C18]
- **Opportunity**: the record created when a lead is qualified as a genuine prospective sale, used to track the deal. [C19]
- **Pipeline** and **stage**: a pipeline shows a sales process as stages, each saying where a deal stands, and each stage can carry a probability that deals in it will close. [C20]
- **Closed won** and **closed lost**: the stages that mark a deal as won or lost. [C21]
- **Quote**: the pricing of what a prospective customer is interested in. [C22]
- **Price objection**: a prospect's concern about the perceived value of what is offered. Sometimes it tests how flexible the price is; sometimes it is a real budget limit. [C23]
- **Concession**: a point on which a seller is willing to give ground in a negotiation. [C24]
- **SPIN**: four kinds of discovery question — Situation, Problem, Implication and Need-payoff. [C25]
- **CRM** (customer relationship management): how a company organises and tracks what it knows about its current and potential customers. [C26]

## Workflows

- The selling process runs in seven steps: prospect and qualify, preapproach, approach, presentation, overcome objections, close, follow up. [C27]
- Qualify before going further: are they interested, can they pay, and does this person have the authority to buy? A prospect with a budget and a clear decision timeline becomes an opportunity to work toward closing. [C28]
- Disqualify a lead that does not meet the criteria, and keep an audit trail of it. [C29]
- Before contacting a customer, check the CRM for earlier contacts and what is already known about them and their business. [C30]
- Find every person involved in the buying decision, and where each one sits in it. [C31]
- A quote starts as a draft and is made active when it is ready to go to the customer. An accepted quote becomes an order; otherwise it is closed as revised, cancelled or lost. [C32]
- Meet a price objection by showing the value: people buy when they see it. [C33]
- Record each customer interaction — calls, emails, meetings — against the deal, and set a reminder for the next follow-up. [C34]
- Close the deal as won or lost when the outcome is known; a lost deal records the reason, the competitor if there was one, and the date. [C35]
- Reopen a closed deal if the client's circumstances change. [C36]
- After the sale, follow up inside the company and with the client to make sure what was sold is delivered as promised. [C37]

## Principles

- Never lie to a customer: a long-term relationship rests on trust and honesty. [C38]
- Withholding information is as much a lie as falsifying it. [C39]
- Promise only what will be kept; a broken promise costs trust. [C40]
- Be honest about timing, and about when and how a problem will be put right. [C41]
- Recommend what fits the customer's needs and what the company can and cannot actually do. [C42]
- Listen to the customer's needs and concerns, before the sale and after it. [C43]
- Qualify properly, because a sale cannot be closed with the wrong prospect; but do not write a lead off until it is clear it cannot qualify. [C44]
- Negotiate for a result that works for both sides, and do not give a concession without getting one in return. [C45]
- Be dependable: reliable, responsible and consistent in meeting obligations. [C46]
- The customer's experience is shaped by operations as well as by the sale. [C47]

## Systems

- The CRM is the customer database: the details of each transaction and the customer's contact information. [C48]
- Notes in the CRM are shared, so everyone in the company who deals with the customer knows what was said. [C49]
- Typical tools are a CRM such as Salesforce, sales and marketing software such as HubSpot, and spreadsheets. [C50]
- Quotes are priced from the company's price list. [C51]
- A pipeline or funnel view shows how deals are spread across the stages and where they are stuck. [C52]
- Most people in the role use email and the telephone every day. [C53]

## Decisions that go to a manager

- Price schedules and discount rates. [C54]
- Customer complaints that the sales staff escalate. [C55]
- Sales goals for the team, and how progress toward them is tracked. [C56]
- Help with a presentation or with closing a sale. [C57]
- Where the sales effort should focus, based on what customers prefer. [C58]

## Measures

- Sales against quota: the sales expected from each representative over a period, compared with the sales actually made. [C59]
- Closing ratio: how often a prospect is turned into a customer. [C60]
- Outcome measures such as sales and new accounts. [C61]
- Weighted pipeline: the amount in each stage multiplied by that stage's probability of closing. [C62]
- Closed deals, won and lost, feed the sales forecast and quota attainment. [C63]
- Why deals are lost, and to which competitors. [C64]
- Deals stalled at a stage, reviewed by the manager. [C65]
- The cost of selling against the revenue it brings, and the profitability of what is sold. [C66]
