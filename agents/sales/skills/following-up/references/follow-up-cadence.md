# The follow-up cadence

Three weeks, four touches, then a decision. The dates are counted from the day the quote was sent.

| When | What goes out | What it adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day 0 | The quote, with the scope and the exclusions | The price, the visit count, what is not included |
| Day 3 | A short email | One thing they asked about, answered; who has the quote if it is gated |
| Day 10 | A phone call, then a note | A question they can answer in one line: what is still open? |
| Day 18 | One last email | The quote's validity date, and what happens if it lapses |
| Day 21 | Close the lead as lost | The reason, in the note, in their words |

## What each touch is for

- **Day 3** is for the client who read the quote and has a question they have not asked. Make it easy
  to ask: name the two things people usually query — what is in scope, and when it could start (and
  that operations answers the second one).
- **Day 10** is the only phone call. If it goes to voicemail, the message is one sentence and the note
  says it went to voicemail. Do not call twice on the same day.
- **Day 18** carries the expiry, because a quote with no expiry is a quote nobody has to answer.
- **Day 21** is a close, not a goodbye: say the quote can be re-opened when the timing is right, and
  log why it went quiet.

## When the cadence restarts

A client who comes back a month later has a new decision to make, not an old one: re-qualify what
changed (the building, the budget year, the contract they were in) and quote again only if the scope
still holds. Do not re-send the old quote — the prices may have moved, and re-sending an expired
quote teaches the client that the dates are decorative.

## Cadence for a lead that was never quoted

An enquiry that never became a quote gets two touches, not four: the answer to their question, and
one follow-up a week later. If neither produces a measured size or a walkthrough, close it. A lead
with no facts is not a lead; it is an email address.
