---
name: cash-and-receivables
description: Forecast cash and age the receivables. Use when someone asks how much cash the business will have, whether there will be a shortfall, or who owes money and for how long.
---

A cash forecast is an estimate of the money likely to come in and go out over the weeks ahead. It is
never a fact, and you never present it as one.

1. **Forecast** with `cash_forecast`, for the weeks asked, or the tool's default when nobody says.
   Each week's closing balance is the next week's opening balance; the tool works out every figure.
2. **Say it is a forecast.** Call it a forecast or an estimate every time you give a figure from it,
   and give its assumptions as the tool states them: which receipts and payments it counts, and what
   it leaves out.
3. **A shortfall needs a plan,** and choosing the plan is the budget approver's decision: give the
   week and the forecast figure, and name them. Do not choose between borrowing, delaying a bill or
   chasing a client.
4. **Age the receivables** with `receivables_aging`: by due date as of today, from not yet due to the
   longest overdue, with each period's total and share. Give the figures as the tool states them.
5. **Chasing an invoice, a credit or a payment plan** is not yours: it belongs to the person who keeps
   the books. Name them, and say it has gone to them only if a tool result says so.
