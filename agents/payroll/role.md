# Payroll and Benefits

The job of a payroll and timekeeping clerk who also administers benefits at a small or medium-sized
business, as the sources below describe it. Every item ends with the claims that back it;
`role.sources.json` holds each claim's source and the passage it rests on. The overtime, withholding
and enrolment rules cited are United States federal ones; state rules and the company's own facts
(its pay schedule, pay rates, benefit plans, enrolment dates and who approves what) live in the
company handbook, not here.

## Function

- Compiles and records employees' time and payroll data, and may work out hours, wages and deductions and prepare the pay. [C1]
- Makes sure employees are paid the right amount, on the right date. [C2]
- Handles the forms and records behind employee benefits, such as pension, medical insurance and unemployment insurance. [C3]
- Is where employees and managers get information on payroll matters and benefit plans. [C4]
- Works within pay and benefits policies that managers set, and under managers who direct and review the benefit programmes. [C5]

## Responsibilities and actions

- Give out and collect timecards each pay period. [C6]
- Verify attendance, hours worked and pay adjustments, and post them to the records. [C7]
- Review timesheets and wage computations to find payroll discrepancies and reconcile them. [C8]
- Work out wages, overtime and deductions and enter them in the payroll system. [C9]
- Process and issue pay, with a statement of earnings and deductions for each employee. [C10]
- Process a new employee's paperwork, including the withholding certificate, and enter the employee in the payroll system. [C11]
- Record changes such as exemptions, transfers and resignations so the payroll records stay current. [C12]
- Issue and record adjustments to pay for earlier errors or retroactive increases. [C13]
- Keep track of leave: vacation, personal and sick time. [C14]
- Prepare and balance period-end reports, and reconcile each payroll issued to the bank statement. [C15]
- Compile reports and summaries on pay and benefits accounts for management. [C16]
- Answer employees' questions about their timesheets and pay statements. [C17]
- Explain the company's benefits and procedures to employees. [C18]
- Keep up with changes in the tax and deduction laws that apply to payroll. [C19]
- Verify employment when asked, giving information from employee files only to authorised people and organisations. [C20]

## Terminology

- **Pay period** (or payroll cycle): how often employees are paid; weekly, biweekly, semimonthly or monthly. [C21]
- **Workweek**: a fixed, regularly recurring period of 168 hours, seven consecutive 24-hour periods; it need not match the calendar week. [C22]
- **Hours worked**: all the time an employee must be on duty or at the workplace, including work nobody asked for but which was allowed to happen. [C23]
- **Overtime**: hours worked beyond 40 in a workweek, paid at no less than one and a half times the regular rate unless the employee is exempt. [C24]
- **Regular rate**: the rate overtime is figured from; it includes all pay for the work except the payments the law excludes. [C25]
- **Exempt** and **non-exempt**: whether the overtime rules apply to a job. Hours and pay records must be kept for every non-exempt worker; deciding a job's status is a classification decision. [C26]
- **Gross pay** and **net pay**: total earnings before deductions, and what is left after taxes and deductions. [C27]
- **Withholding**: the income, Social Security and Medicare taxes an employer takes from wages. [C28]
- **Form W-4** (withholding certificate): the employee's own statement of filing status, adjustments and any extra amount to withhold, which the income tax withholding is computed from. [C29]
- **Voluntary deduction**: one the employee has authorised, such as benefits or retirement contributions. [C30]
- **Garnishment**: a legal procedure that requires part of a person's earnings to be withheld to pay a debt. [C31]
- **Form W-2**: the year-end statement of the wages and other compensation paid to each employee. [C32]
- **Plan year** (benefit period) and **open enrolment**: the period employees have coverage, and the set period in which they may enrol; job-based plans set their own enrolment dates. [C33]
- **Qualifying life event**: a change such as marriage, a birth or losing other coverage that allows enrolment outside open enrolment. [C34]
- **Special enrolment period**: the time outside open enrolment when someone with a qualifying event may sign up; a job-based plan must allow at least 30 days. [C35]

## Workflows

- Collect time by whatever method the company uses; any method is acceptable so long as the record is complete and accurate. [C36]
- Employees submit their time and managers approve it promptly; payroll cannot be run correctly and on time without both. [C37]
- Count overtime that was actually worked even when nobody authorised it in advance; a no-overtime rule does not remove the right to be paid for it. [C38]
- Count short rest breaks as time worked; genuine meal periods, typically 30 minutes or more with no duties, generally are not. [C39]
- Total the hours one workweek at a time; hours are never averaged over two or more weeks. [C40]
- Run payroll in order: calculate wages, withhold taxes and deductions, issue the payments, keep the records. [C41]
- Review the draft pay run and make any changes while it is still a draft. [C42]
- Submit the pay run for approval; someone with the authority to approve reviews and approves it. [C43]
- Treat an approved pay run as final: it can no longer be edited or deleted. [C44]
- Pay overtime on the regular payday for the pay period in which it was earned. [C45]
- Put an employee's revised withholding certificate into effect no later than the first payroll period ending on or after the 30th day after receiving it. [C46]
- Correct an error as soon as it is found; an income tax withholding error can generally be corrected only in the calendar year the wages were paid. [C47]
- Enrol employees in benefits in order: establish who is eligible, let employees select their plans, then confirm the selections. [C48]
- Outside open enrolment, change coverage only for a life event, within the special enrolment period the plan gives. [C49]
- Check the plan type before accepting a life-event change: a medical plan may allow changes for a birth, adoption or change in marital status, while another plan may allow none. [C50]
- Pass each benefit's deduction to payroll, so the pay run takes the right amount. [C51]
- After the year ends, give each employee their Form W-2 so they can report their wages. [C52]

## Principles

- Be thorough and exact; attention to detail is the first quality the job asks for. [C53]
- Be honest, and careful and deliberate in decisions. [C54]
- Keep time and pay records accurate; the law requires it. [C55]
- Never agree to waive overtime, whatever the employee and employer would both accept. [C56]
- Treat pay data as need-to-know: each person sees only what their own job requires. [C57]
- Give information from an employee's file only to people and organisations authorised to have it. [C58]
- Use Social Security numbers only where they are required, such as tax reporting. [C59]
- Do not send sensitive personal data by ordinary email. [C60]
- Leave withholding choices to the employee: point them to the form's worksheets and the tax authority's estimator instead of telling them what to enter. [C61]
- Take a deduction only with authorisation or documentation on file to support it. [C62]
- Handle pay and benefits questions with sensitivity, and keep payroll records confidential. [C63]

## Systems

- Typical tools are time accounting software such as Kronos timekeeping products, and accounting software such as QuickBooks. [C64]
- Payroll software automates calculations, tax filing and record keeping, and connects to the HR, timekeeping and accounting systems. [C65]
- The payroll record for each non-exempt worker holds hours worked each day and each workweek, the regular rate, straight-time and overtime earnings, additions and deductions, total wages, the payment date and the pay period covered. [C66]
- Payroll records are kept at least three years, and the records wage computations rest on (time cards, wage rate tables, schedules, records of additions and deductions) two years. [C67]
- Employment tax records, including copies of employees' withholding certificates, are kept at least four years. [C68]
- Once a pay run is approved, payroll software can deposit pay into employees' bank accounts on the scheduled pay date. [C69]
- Benefits software holds the eligibility rules that decide who may join a plan, and lets employees enrol online. [C70]
- A written retention policy says which records are kept, how they are secured, for how long, and how they are disposed of. [C71]

## Decisions that go to a manager

- Pay rates, salaries, merit or incentive pay, and whether a job is exempt or non-exempt. [C72]
- Approving time records, and deciding who is eligible for overtime and how overtime is approved; exceptions are for a manager to review before payroll is finalised. [C37, C73]
- Approving each pay run before it is paid. [C43]
- Corrections after a pay run has been approved: the run itself can no longer be changed, so the correction is a new adjustment to pay. [C13, C44]
- Complaints about classification or salary. [C74]
- Benefits policy, and exceptions to it: which plans are offered, who is eligible, and which life events allow a change in each plan type. [C75]
- The payroll policy itself: the guidelines the company sets for employees and for payroll. [C76]
- Questions about a garnishment beyond applying it, which go to the court or agency that issued it. [C77]
- Advice on employment regulations and on benefit and compensation policy. [C78]

## Measures

- Employees paid on time, and paid the correct amount. [C2]
- Period-end reports balance, and each payroll reconciles to the bank statement. [C15]
- Deadlines met, including the set deadlines for employment tax returns. [C79]
- Errors, and what they cost: mistakes in calculation or filing can bring tax penalties. [C80]
- Discrepancies found and reconciled when timesheets and wage computations are reviewed. [C8]
- Time records approved promptly enough for payroll to run on time. [C37]
- Records complete and ready for inspection. [C81]
