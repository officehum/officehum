# Quality

The job of a quality control inspector or quality coordinator at a small or medium-sized business,
as the sources below describe it. It covers a services business, where delivered work is inspected
against a checklist, as well as a producer. Every item ends with the claims that back it;
`role.sources.json` holds each claim's source and the passage it rests on. The company's own facts
(its standards, checklists, pass marks, customers and who may approve what) live in the company
handbook, not here.

## Function

- Checks that the company's products and services meet its quality standards. [C1]
- Inspects, tests or measures materials, products, installations or finished work to see whether they conform to the specification. [C2]
- Controls quality so that only work that meets the customer's requirements is offered to the customer for acceptance. [C3]
- Records what each inspection found and reports the findings to managers. [C4]
- Works inside the quality programme that a quality manager plans and directs; the manager sets the quality policy. [C5]

## Responsibilities and actions

- Read the specification, checklist or procedure to find out what is to be inspected and how. [C6]
- Inspect at each stage: incoming materials, work in progress and finished work. [C7]
- Record the inspection data and how much was inspected. [C8]
- Accept or reject each item, and mark it with its acceptance or rejection status. [C9]
- Take out of use whatever fails to meet the specification. [C10]
- Write an inspection report giving the results, any recommendations and the repairs needed. [C11]
- Tell supervisors and the other people concerned about the problems found. [C12]
- Recommend corrective actions based on the inspection results. [C13]
- Help analyse and correct the problem behind a defect. [C14]
- Work out defect percentages or averages with set formulas. [C15]
- Review and evaluate every complaint to decide whether it needs an investigation. [C16]
- Assess whether a change made to a process actually worked. [C17]

## Terminology

- **Acceptance activities**: the inspections, tests and other checks that verify work before it is accepted. [C18]
- **Quality requirements**: the technical requirements a contract sets for the quality of a product or service, with the inspections and other controls the supplier must carry out. [C19]
- **Nonconformance** (NC): a product or piece of work that does not conform to the specified requirements. [C20]
- **Critical**, **major** and **minor** nonconformance: one likely to cause a hazard or unsafe conditions; one likely to make the work fail or materially less usable; one unlikely to reduce its usability much. [C21, C22, C23]
- **Disposition**: what is decided about nonconforming work, for example that it is unusable or may be used with restrictions; the decision is documented. [C24]
- **Rework** (for a service, **re-performance**): doing the work again so that it conforms, followed by a fresh inspection. [C25]
- **Conditional acceptance**: accepting work that does not conform, or is incomplete, on condition that it is corrected or completed by a set date. [C26]
- **Containment** (interim containment action): a temporary action that protects the customer until the permanent fix is in place, and is normally removed after it. [C27]
- **Root cause analysis** (RCA): a structured investigation, starting with data collection and a reconstruction of what happened, to find the cause that must be removed for a permanent fix. [C28]
- **5 Whys**: asking why again and again to get past the obvious symptoms to the root cause. [C29]
- **8D** (eight disciplines): a team-based problem-solving method that finds the root cause, contains the problem to protect customers, and takes corrective action against similar problems in future. [C30]
- **Corrective action** and **preventive action** (CAPA): the steps that fix a problem and stop it recurring, and the steps that stop a problem from happening in the first place. [C31]

## Workflows

- Inspect against the template: an inspection is a set of individual checks, and a check whose reading does not match its acceptance criterion is rejected. [C32]
- Record every inspection: what was checked, the date, the results and who did it. [C33]
- When work does not conform, identify it, document it, evaluate it, keep it apart from good work and decide its disposition. [C34]
- As part of that evaluation, decide whether an investigation is needed and notify the people responsible for the nonconformance. [C35]
- Contain first: take an interim action that protects the customer before the permanent fix is known, and check that the containment works. [C36]
- Correct the work itself: nonconforming work is normally corrected or replaced, where that can be done within the agreed schedule. [C37]
- Find the root cause: keep asking why until the root cause is reached; five is not a fixed number. [C38]
- Use the 8D sequence for a serious or repeated problem, such as a customer complaint or an unacceptable level of rejects: describe the problem, contain it, find the root cause and the point where the problem slipped through, choose the permanent corrective action, implement and validate it, prevent recurrence, then close. [C39, C40]
- Aim the corrective action at the root cause, so that it changes the conditions that produced the problem. [C41]
- Verify that the action is effective, and has no harmful side effect, before treating the problem as solved; use a measurement to show the improvement. [C42]
- Record the changes made to methods and procedures, and update the procedures and work instructions. [C43]
- At closure, file the record and write down the lessons learned. [C44]
- Handle complaints in a uniform and timely way, and write down a spoken complaint when it is received. [C45]
- Investigate any complaint that the work may not have met its specification, unless a similar complaint has already been investigated and another investigation is unnecessary. [C46]
- When a complaint is not investigated, record the reason and who made that decision. [C47]
- Look across the records (inspection results, quality records, service records, concessions, complaints and returned work) for existing and potential causes of quality problems. [C48]

## Principles

- Offer for acceptance only work that conforms, and expect a customer to reject work that does not conform in every respect. [C3, C49]
- Look for the underlying problems that make errors likely, not for an individual to blame. [C50]
- Treat the cause, not the symptom: a fix that only replaces what broke lets the failure recur. [C51]
- Be wary of weak corrective actions, such as retraining or restating an existing rule, as the only fix. [C52]
- Document every corrective and preventive activity and its result. [C53]
- Be thorough and attentive to detail; the job depends on noticing flaws and deficiencies. [C54]
- Treat repeated nonconformances as a problem in themselves, even when each one is minor. [C55]

## Systems

- Inspection and checklist tools score an inspection from weighted questions, so that the more important questions are worth more points. [C56]
- A change to a published checklist template applies only to new inspections, not to ones already done. [C57]
- A quality system can block a delivery or receipt until its inspection has been done. [C58]
- An inspection record names who inspected the work and who verified the inspection. [C59]
- A quality alert or nonconformance record notifies the quality team of a defect or other issue; it names the person responsible and the root cause once known, and holds the corrective and preventive actions. [C60, C31]
- A nonconformance record gives the quantity affected, the source and type of the problem and explanatory notes; classifying problem types makes later analysis easier. [C61]
- A complaint investigation record holds the nature and details of the complaint, the dates and results of the investigation, any corrective action taken and any reply to the complainant. [C62]
- Complete records of all inspection work are kept, and are available to the customer for as long as the contract requires. [C63]

## Decisions that go to a manager

- The disposition of nonconforming work: who reviews it and who has authority to decide is set by procedure. [C64]
- Using or accepting nonconforming work as it is: the record carries the justification and the signature of the person who authorised the use. [C65]
- Asking a customer to accept nonconforming or incomplete work, and any adjustment offered in return. [C66]
- A price reduction or other remedy for a customer: it reflects the reduced value of the work and changes the contract. [C67]
- Approval of the changes a corrective action requires, and review of quality problems and of corrective and preventive actions: the sponsor gives final approval, and the information goes to management review. [C68, C69]
- The decision not to investigate a complaint, which is recorded against a named person. [C47]
- Stopping production or service delivery when serious defects are present. [C70]
- Matters about individual staff: overseeing and instructing the people who do and inspect the work is the quality manager's job. [C71]
- The inspection criteria, checklists and quality procedures themselves, which the quality manager creates, reviews and updates. [C72]

## Measures

- Inspection score, and the average score across inspections: the sum of the score percentages divided by the number of inspections. [C73]
- Defect percentages or averages. [C15]
- Nonconformances over time, by type of problem, and the solutions that were applied to them. [C74, C61]
- The cost of managing a nonconformance: materials, charges and the hours spent correcting it. [C75]
- Whether past quality measures were effective, judged from historical data. [C76]
- Regular reports on nonconformances, daily quality, root cause analyses and quality trends. [C77]
