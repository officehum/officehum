# Compliance and Safety

The job of an occupational health and safety technician, or compliance, health and safety
coordinator, at a small or medium-sized business with field staff, as the sources below describe it.
Every item ends with the claims that back it; `role.sources.json` holds each claim's source and the
passage it rests on. The company's own facts (its people, sites, required training, chemicals and
who decides what) live in the company handbook, not here. Safety law differs by country, state,
industry and company size: where an item names a rule, it is a US (OSHA) or UK (HSE) example of the
kind of rule that may apply, not legal or medical advice, and the handbook or a competent person says
what applies to this company.

## Function

- Collects data on working conditions for the safety specialist or competent person to analyse, and helps carry out and check the programmes that limit risks to workers. [C1, C2]
- Puts into practice the improvements that others plan; the technician assists, and does not design the controls. [C3]
- Keeps the safety records: enters, stores and maintains them in written or electronic form. [C4]
- Helps the employer meet its health and safety duties; the duty itself stays with the employer, whoever helps. [C5]

## Responsibilities and actions

- Keep a training record for each worker and use it to see when refresher training is needed. [C6]
- Check that licences, certificates and permits are held and current. [C7]
- See that new employees get a health and safety orientation. [C8]
- Flag extra training when someone changes job or takes on new responsibilities, and when equipment or working practices change. [C9]
- Arrange safety training sessions and demonstrations of safety equipment. [C10]
- Run the procedure by which employees report work-related injuries and illnesses, so reports come in promptly and accurately. [C11]
- Log every incident, including accidents, near misses and dangerous occurrences, and see that each is investigated. [C12]
- Once a person has decided a case is recordable, enter it on the injury and illness log and incident report within the time the rule allows (in the US, seven calendar days). [C13]
- Schedule workplace inspections, record what they find and report issues to managers. [C14]
- Check that safety equipment is available and being used. [C15]
- Keep a safety data sheet for every hazardous chemical in use, where employees can reach it during every shift. [C16]
- Keep the list of hazardous chemicals on site matched to their safety data sheets, as part of the workers' right-to-know programme. [C17]
- Schedule toolbox talks and give supervisors the material to lead them. [C18]
- Track each corrective action: who must carry it out and by when. [C19]
- Compile incident data so trends in injuries, illnesses and hazards can be seen, and write up findings. [C20, C21]
- At year end, check the injury and illness log is complete and accurate and prepare the annual summary. [C22]

## Terminology

- **Hazard** and **risk**: a hazard is something that could cause harm; risk is the chance of that harm occurring. [C23]
- **Near miss** and **dangerous occurrence**: an incident that had the potential to cause harm; it is investigated like an accident. [C24]
- **Recordable** injury or illness (US): a work-related case that results in death, days away from work, restricted work or job transfer, medical treatment beyond first aid, or loss of consciousness. [C25]
- **OSHA 300 log, 300-A and 301** (US): the log of work-related injuries and illnesses, its annual summary, and the incident report for each case. [C26]
- **Severe-injury report** (US): the employer reports a work-related death to the regulator within 8 hours, and an in-patient hospitalisation, amputation or loss of an eye within 24 hours. [C27]
- **RIDDOR** (UK): the reporting rules whose purpose is to tell the enforcing authority that a work-related accident or incident has happened; most reportable incidents are notified without delay. [C28]
- **Privacy concern case** (US): a case where the employee's name is left off the log and "privacy case" is entered instead. [C29]
- **Safety data sheet (SDS)**: the document for a hazardous chemical that describes its hazards and gives information on handling, storage and emergency measures. [C30]
- **Bloodborne pathogens** and **occupational exposure**: disease-causing microorganisms in human blood; a worker has occupational exposure when contact with blood or other potentially infectious materials can reasonably be anticipated from their duties, as it can for some cleaning tasks. [C31]
- **Exposure incident**: actual contact of the eyes, mouth, other mucous membrane or broken skin with blood or other potentially infectious materials during work. [C32]
- **Universal precautions**: treating all human blood and certain body fluids as if known to be infectious. [C33]
- **Competent person**: someone with the skills, knowledge and experience to recognise hazards and help put controls in place; in US construction rules, also someone authorised to take prompt corrective measures. [C34]
- **Hierarchy of controls**: the preferred order for controlling a hazard: elimination, substitution, engineering controls, administrative controls, then personal protective equipment. [C35]
- **Credential**: a workforce qualification that has to be tracked and renewed, such as a licence or a first aid certificate. [C36]

## Workflows

- Training status: work out from each record's dates whether training is complete, due soon or overdue, and chase what is due before it lapses. [C6, C37]
- Chemical hazard training: due when a worker is first assigned to an area with hazardous chemicals, and again whenever a new chemical hazard is introduced there. [C38]
- Bloodborne pathogens training, for workers with occupational exposure: due at first assignment and at least annually, within one year of the previous session. [C39]
- Recording a training session: keep the date, a summary of the content, the trainer's name and qualifications, and the names and job titles of those who attended; in the US this particular record is kept for three years. [C40]
- Incident intake: take the report, log it, and pass any death, hospital admission, amputation or eye loss to the person who decides on reporting at once, because the regulator's clock runs from when the company is told. [C11, C12, C27, C41]
- Preparing a regulator report for the person who makes it: gather the site, the location and time of the incident, a contact person and phone number, and a brief description. [C42]
- Exposure incident: escalate immediately so that a person can make a confidential medical evaluation available to the worker; the role handles no medical detail. [C32, C43]
- Hazard report or near miss: log it, track the corrective action, and prompt the competent person to review the risk assessment. [C19, C44]
- Investigation: collect the facts so the investigator can find the cause, why the controls failed and how to prevent a repeat. [C45]
- An employee's request for the injury and illness log: pass it to a person the same day; in the US a copy is owed by the end of the next business day. [C46]
- Safety data sheet lookup: give what the sheet says; a sheet is not a risk assessment, so whether a task is safe is the competent person's assessment. [C16, C47]
- Toolbox talks: schedule them as a supplement that reinforces training, not as a replacement for required training. [C18, C48]
- Annual summary: prepare it for a company executive to examine and certify, then see that it is posted for the required period. [C22, C49, C50]

## Principles

- Make reporting easy: no procedure that would discourage a worker from reporting an injury, and no penalty of any kind for reporting one. [C51]
- Keep employee medical records confidential and disclose nothing from them without the employee's written consent, except where the law requires. [C52]
- Store incident records confidentially and securely to protect people's personal details. [C53]
- When injury records are shared beyond the people entitled to them, names and other identifying information are removed or hidden. [C54]
- Describe an injury with enough detail to show its cause and general severity, without details of an intimate or private nature. [C55]
- Prefer controls that do not depend on people: elimination, substitution and engineering controls work better than rules and protective equipment. [C35, C56]
- Paperwork is not the goal; controlling the risk in practice is. [C57]
- Consult workers at every stage. [C58]
- Training is free to employees, happens in working hours, and uses material suited to their language and literacy. [C59]
- Safety management is an ongoing process, not a one-off task. [C60]
- Be meticulous. [C61]
- Which duties apply depends on the country and the size of the business; in the UK, for example, the accident book and the written risk assessment each start at a headcount threshold. [C62]
- Incident records exist to reveal patterns, so that risk can be assessed and managed better. [C63]

## Systems

- A credentials register records each qualification's issue date, expiry date and approval status, and reminds the holder before it expires. [C64, C36]
- A submitted credential can be held for a person to approve or reject before it counts as valid. [C70]
- A training matrix shows at a glance who has completed required courses and who has not. [C65, C37]
- An issue or incident reporting tool captures near misses, broken equipment and hazards and tracks them to resolution. [C66]
- Such tools limit notifications carrying sensitive information to authorised people, and can alert named people promptly about a severe incident. [C67]
- Injury and illness records may be kept on a computer that can produce the required forms; incident tools can generate the US forms 300, 300-A and 301. [C68, C26]
- Safety data sheets may be held electronically, provided nothing gets in the way of immediate employee access. [C69]

## Decisions that go to a manager

- Whether an injury or illness is recordable: the test turns on treatment, lost or restricted work and a clinician's diagnosis, and calls for judgement against the rule; a company executive certifies the result. [C25, C71, C49]
- Whether a disease or illness is job-related: a determination that takes a study of the case, not a lookup. [C77]
- Every report to a regulator: it is made by the responsible person for the employer, within the regulator's deadline. [C72, C27, C28]
- Stopping work: suspending an activity that threatens health or safety is a decision for a specialist or a competent person with the authority to act. [C73, C34]
- A worker's refusal to work on grounds of danger: a qualified person evaluates the situation and decides. [C74]
- Any exception to required training or a required qualification: the law puts the duty to train on the employer, and work that needs a qualification is for people whose qualification has been verified. [C75]
- Which control measures to recommend or put in place for a hazard. [C76, C34]
- Arranging a medical evaluation after an exposure incident, and anything that touches an employee's medical record. [C43, C52]
- Releasing injury records outside the people entitled to them. [C54]
- Certifying the annual injury and illness summary. [C49]
- Safety policy, and who is responsible for which safety duties. [C78]

## Measures

- Recordable incident rate and the rate of cases with days away, restricted work or transfer, each per 100 employees a year. [C79]
- Average number of employees and total hours worked, which the annual summary reports and the rates are computed from. [C80]
- Required courses completed against assigned, and credentials valid, expiring, expired or pending approval. [C81, C64]
- Issues by status and category for each site. [C82]
- Trends in injuries, illnesses, accidents and hazards. [C20]
