# Inventory and Warehouse

The job of a stock clerk, inventory controller or storeroom and warehouse coordinator at a small or
medium-sized business, as the sources below describe it: receiving goods and putting them away,
issuing stock, moving it between locations, counting it, and keeping the stock record in agreement
with what is on the shelf. Every item ends with the claims that back it; `role.sources.json` holds
each claim's source and the passage it rests on. The company's own facts (its items, locations,
reorder levels, count schedule, tolerances and who approves what) live in the company handbook, not
here. This role keeps the stock record and asks for replenishment; choosing suppliers and placing
orders is the Purchasing role's job. Where a source describes US federal property rules, US or UK
safety rules, or one vendor's software, it is an example of sound practice, not a rule every
business must follow.

## Function

- Receives, stores and issues merchandise, materials, equipment and other items from a stockroom, warehouse or storage yard. [C1]
- Tracks product information so that the business stays on schedule, and looks after recordkeeping and inventory control. [C2]
- Counts the stock: a properly run physical count confirms that the goods exist and that the records are complete and accurate. [C3]
- Raises requisitions for stock that is running low, based on the space available, the stock on hand and demand. [C4]
- Works under a stock supervisor, shift supervisor or warehouse manager, who leads the team and forecasts stock levels. [C5]

## Responsibilities and actions

- Receive and count stock items, and record the data by hand or on a computer. [C6]
- Check what arrived against the manifest, invoice or order, and check the labelling and paperwork and for damaged or missing items. [C7, C8]
- Record damages and discrepancies in a shipment, and take them up with the people who can put them right. [C9, C10]
- Store items in an orderly and accessible way, and decide the storage method, identification and stock location from turnover, environmental factors and what the facility can physically hold. [C11, C12]
- Issue materials, parts and supplies to coworkers or customers against incoming requisitions. [C13]
- Keep records of items shipped, received or transferred to another location, and update the stock information in the system. [C14, C15]
- Take inventory to identify the items that need to be reordered or replenished. [C16]
- Carry out regular inventory checks and stock audits, and check the inventory records for accuracy. [C17, C18]
- Replace out-of-date items on shelves and racks. [C19]
- Inspect stock for wear or defects and report any damage to a supervisor. [C20]
- Dispose of damaged or defective items or return them to the vendor. [C21]
- Make sure items are stored safely, and keep storage areas clean and in line with safety regulations. [C22]
- Compile reports on changes in inventory and on materials held and used. [C23]

## Terminology

- **Perpetual inventory**: a system that keeps current item balances by recording each receipt and shipment. [C24]
- **Cycle count**: counting a portion of the inventory daily, weekly or monthly until all of it has been counted over a period of time. [C25]
- **Wall-to-wall count**: counting the entire inventory at one point in time, usually at the end of an annual or interim period. [C26]
- **Blind count**: a count made without knowing, or having access to, the on-hand quantity in the records. [C27]
- **Variance**: a discrepancy between the physical count and the on-hand balance; investigating it is called research. [C28]
- **Tolerance**: the range within which a counted quantity may disagree with the record and still be treated as accurate. [C29]
- **Cutoff**: controlling the movement of items between locations during a count, and making sure each movement and its quantity change in the system line up in time. [C30]
- **Quantity adjustment**: in inventory software, for example, an entry made when the recorded stock does not match the actual stock for reasons outside normal business, such as theft, damage, a data entry error or a write-off. [C31]
- **Reorder point**: in inventory systems, for example, the stock level that represents demand during the lead time; when stock is projected to fall below it, it is time to order more. [C32]
- **Maximum level**: in inventory systems, for example, the level that an order is sized to reach: the order quantity is the gap between projected stock and the maximum. [C33]
- **Transfer order**: in inventory systems, for example, a document that ships stock out of one location and receives it at another; the items are unavailable while in transit. [C34]
- **First-expired-first-out (FEFO)**: in warehouse systems, for example, picking the items with the earliest expiry dates first. [C35]
- **Safety data sheet**: a supplier's document that describes a chemical's hazards and gives information on handling, storage and emergency measures. [C36]
- **Inventory record accuracy**: the degree of agreement between the balance in the records and the physical count. [C37]

## Workflows

- Receive and put away: compare the shipment with the order, then record the quantities received and the bin each item goes to. In US federal property rules, for example, overages, shortages and damage found on receipt must be acted on. [C7, C38, C39]
- Issue stock: issue against a requisition, so that the record for each item shows the quantity received, the quantity issued and the balance on hand. [C13, C40]
- Transfer between locations: in inventory systems, for example, a transfer posts an issue at the "from" location and a receipt at the "to" location. Stock found in the wrong location is corrected with a transfer, not written into the count as a difference. [C41, C42]
- Plan cycle counts: count the most important items most often and the less important ones least often, for example the more valuable items. [C43]
- Prepare for a count: tidy the storage locations, separate and label the items, check that items are in the right location, and identify excess and obsolete stock. [C44]
- Control movement during a cycle count: count in a slow period, stop movement of the items selected for that day, or use the transaction history to reconcile the count. [C45]
- Count blind: the counter is given the part number, description and location, but not the quantity on record. [C46]
- Recount before changing anything: record balances are typically not adjusted until recounts and research are complete, and a recount can be triggered by any variance, by a variance over the tolerance, or by a supervisor's judgment. [C47, C48]
- Research a variance promptly: review the transaction history and the shipping and receiving records, ideally on the day of the count or the day after, and give each error a cause code. [C49, C50, C51]
- Adjust the record: adjustments are routinely referred to management for approval, and each one carries a reason. In warehouse systems, for example, a count that differs waits in a pending-review status and is rejected unless the difference is approved. [C52, C53, C54]
- Small variances: management may set a low value or quantity tolerance under which the balance is adjusted without research. [C55]
- Handle damaged, defective or expired stock: take it out of use, record it, and dispose of it or return it to the vendor. In inventory systems, for example, an item with a known defect can be blocked from every transaction, and some clerks decide whether a defective product is scrapped or sent back. [C20, C21, C56, C57]
- Run a wall-to-wall count: shut warehouse operations down for the count, and cover stock on the receiving dock, in the warehouse, in the shipping area and at outside locations. [C58, C59]
- Receive and store chemicals: under US workplace rules, for example, labels on incoming containers of hazardous chemicals are not removed or defaced, and the safety data sheets are kept where employees can reach them during every shift. [C60, C61]
- Store flammable liquids: in UK safety guidance, for example, containers are kept closed in fire-resisting cabinets or bins that retain spills, apart from oxidisers and corrosive materials. [C62, C63]

## Principles

- Separate the duties: custody of the stock, recording its transactions and approving them are ideally done by different people. [C64]
- Where they cannot be separated, use other controls: blind counts, more supervision, or two people doing the task together. [C65]
- Record every transaction that affects a balance, so that the records are complete, current and auditable. [C66]
- Find and correct the cause of a variance, not only the number. [C67]
- Count what is there: counters are accountable for an accurate count, not for making the count agree with the records. [C68]
- Store material so that it creates no hazard: in US workplace rules, for example, stacked goods are kept stable and limited in height. [C69]
- Keep aisles clear and storage areas free of anything that could cause a trip, fire, explosion or pest problem. [C70]

## Systems

- Clerks use computers, handheld barcode scanners and RFID scanners to keep track of inventory. [C71]
- Typical software includes inventory management and tracking systems and warehouse management systems. [C72]
- In warehouse systems, for example, each worker can be given a maximum percentage or quantity by which a count may deviate before it needs review. [C73]
- In inventory systems, for example, posting an adjustment changes the inventory value and creates ledger transactions. [C74]
- In inventory systems, for example, the system suggests a supply order when stock passes below the reorder point. [C75]

## Decisions that go to a manager

- Approving an adjustment to the stock record: the approval level rises with the value of the adjustment, from the lead or supervisor up to the location manager. Where each level sits is the company's own choice, set in its handbook. [C52, C76]
- Which variances must be researched, and the tolerances that apply. [C77, C55]
- Suspected theft or fraud: adjustments and trends on items susceptible to theft are reviewed in case they must be referred to security or the police. [C78]
- The count approach, which items are counted and how often. [C79]
- Disposing of excess, defective or obsolete stock: staff recommend it, and report damage to a supervisor. [C80, C20]
- Lost stock: in US federal property rules, for example, losses are promptly recognised, investigated and reported. [C81]
- Stock levels: forecasting them is a supervisor's job. [C5]

## Measures

- Inventory record accuracy rate: the number of accurate records divided by the number counted. [C37, C82]
- An accuracy goal: leading companies set it at 95 percent or better. [C83]
- The number and value of adjustments, errors and items counted, and how often each error code occurs. [C84]
- Damages and discrepancies recorded on shipments. [C9]
