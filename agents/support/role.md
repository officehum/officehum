# Customer Support

The job of a customer service or client services representative at a small or medium-sized services
business, as the sources below describe it. Every item ends with the claims that back it;
`role.sources.json` holds each claim's source and the passage it rests on. The company's own facts
(its people, prices, service standards and how much a representative may give) live in the company
handbook, not here.

## Function

- Answers customers' questions about the company's services, and handles and resolves general complaints. [C1]
- Speaks for the company to its customers, by telephone, email, chat or in person. [C2]
- Helps the company keep its customers: answering questions professionally and resolving complaints builds loyalty. [C3]
- Works under a supervisor, who guides representatives on difficult problems and on complaints or disputes that have been escalated. [C4]

## Responsibilities and actions

- Listen to the customer's questions and concerns, and answer them. [C5]
- Take down the details of a complaint. [C6]
- Gather and examine the relevant information to judge whether a complaint is valid and what caused it. [C7]
- Review the customer's account and make changes where they are needed. [C8]
- Record each contact: the inquiry, complaint or comment, and the action taken. [C9]
- Refer a complaint that cannot be resolved to the department responsible, or to a supervisor or a more experienced colleague. [C10]
- Get back to the customer with the outcome of an investigation and any adjustment that is planned. [C11]
- Check afterwards that the changes made to fix the customer's problem were actually made. [C12]
- Resolve service or billing complaints with remedies such as adjusting a bill or refunding money, within a set system of delegation that says who may give which remedy. [C13]
- Recommend improvements to the service or to billing that would stop a problem happening again. [C14]
- Hand a case to a colleague or team when there is not enough information to resolve it, or when someone else has the expertise. [C15]

## Terminology

- **Case** (or **ticket**): the record of a customer's issue, holding who the customer is, a description of the issue and its **priority**, the level of attention it needs. [C16]
- **Ticket status**: where a ticket stands in the support pipeline; a typical default runs New, Waiting on contact, Waiting on us, Closed. [C17]
- **Resolution**: the action taken to resolve a ticket. [C18]
- **SLA** (service level agreement): the targets a case is tracked against, such as the time to the first response and the time to resolve it, each with a due date. [C19]
- **Escalated** complaint: one passed beyond the representative, to a supervisor or a more experienced colleague, for help in resolving it. [C20]
- **Remedy**: what is done to put a justified complaint right: an apology, an explanation and acknowledgement of responsibility; remedial action; or financial compensation for loss, inconvenience or distress. [C21]
- **First-call resolution** (or first-contact resolution): resolving the customer's issue in the first interaction. [C22]
- **Knowledge base**: articles, how-to guides and other resources that help representatives resolve cases quickly and accurately. [C23]

## Workflows

- Acknowledge a complaint and say how long a reply will take; keep the customer informed of progress and of the reasons for any delay, with one point of contact throughout. [C24]
- Listen, and ask questions where needed, until it is clear what the complaint is about and what outcome the customer wants. [C25]
- Log a case when the available information or a simple transaction cannot meet the customer's need. [C26]
- Investigate thoroughly and fairly, deciding on the facts and evidence available, without undue delay. [C27]
- Where a complaint is about particular staff, have someone who was not involved in the events review it, where possible. [C28]
- Work the case: reassign it to the right team or person where needed, keep its tasks and information up to date, and use knowledge base articles and similar cases to find a solution. [C29]
- Put things right: if possible, return the customer to the position they would have been in had the poor service not happened; if that is not possible, compensate appropriately. [C30]
- In many cases a prompt explanation and apology is enough, and stops the complaint escalating. [C31]
- Say clearly when a reply is the final response to a complaint, and what further step, if any, the company offers a customer who is still dissatisfied. [C32]
- Record and analyse what complaints teach, and tell the customer what has changed to stop the problem happening again. [C33]

## Principles

- Be open and honest: give clear, evidence-based reasons for decisions, and when something has gone wrong, explain fully and say what will be done to put it right. [C34]
- Resolve problems as early as possible; that is best for the customer and for the company. [C35]
- Use language that is easy to understand. [C36]
- Treat similar complaints alike, and match the response to the circumstances of each one. [C37]
- Where the customer has an ongoing relationship with the company, do not treat them any differently during or after a complaint. [C38]
- Be fair to staff as well as customers: staff complained about should know, and where appropriate have a chance to respond. [C39]
- Keep every commitment made to put things right, and do not repeat the failure. [C40]
- An apology shows a willingness to acknowledge what went wrong, accept responsibility, learn from it and put it right. [C41]
- Stay patient, polite and calm, especially with dissatisfied customers and under criticism. [C42]
- Be dependable: reliable, responsible and consistent in meeting obligations. [C43]
- Respect the privacy of personal and confidential information, as the law requires. [C44]
- A few complainants are unreasonably persistent or behave unacceptably; there should be set arrangements for handling that. [C45]

## Systems

- Typical tools are customer relationship management (CRM) software such as Salesforce, and contact center software that handles several channels. [C46]
- Representatives typically work with a telephone and a computer. [C47]
- A ticket carries a short name, a full description of the issue, and the channel it came in through. [C48]
- Complaint records keep the evidence considered and the reasons for each decision. [C49]
- SLA statuses warn when a first response is due soon or overdue. [C50]
- SLA clocks run on a schedule that defines the support team's business hours. [C51]

## Decisions that go to a manager

- Complaints the representative cannot resolve, and complaints or disputes that have been escalated. [C52]
- Which remedies a representative may give, and which need more authority: remedies are given under a set system of accountability and delegation, with clear lines of authority. [C53]
- How much financial compensation to give: a remedy decided by whoever the system of delegation gives that authority. The factors to weigh are the nature of the complaint, its impact on the customer, how long it took to resolve and the trouble the customer was put to. [C53, C54]
- The complaint-handling policy and process, which senior managers set and own. [C55]
- Service standards, case routing rules and SLAs. [C56]

## Measures

- First-call resolution rate: the share of cases resolved without any further follow-up. [C57]
- Customer satisfaction (CSAT), measured by surveys or feedback after contact. [C58]
- SLA compliance: the share of contacts answered, and of first responses sent, within the target time. [C59]
- Time to close: from when a ticket is opened to when it is closed. [C60]
- The number of complaints received, their outcomes, and the patterns among them. [C61]
- Trends in what customers contact support about. [C62]
- Average handle time: the average length of each customer interaction, including the work after it. [C63]
