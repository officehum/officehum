# Northwind Facilities Ltd.

Northwind cleans and maintains commercial buildings in Portland, Oregon. It has been doing it since
2011, it has twenty-four people, and almost all of its work is on a contract: the same crew, the same
scope, the same night, every week, with a written list of what a visit covers. The rest is one-off —
a deep clean before a tenant moves in, a floor stripped and re-waxed, a door that will not close.

We are not the cheapest and we do not pretend to be. What clients buy is that a visit happens when
the schedule says it will, that the same two people turn up, and that when something is missed
someone answers the phone the same day and puts it right.

## The services

Six things are on sale, and every one of them is priced from the price book:

1. **Recurring janitorial** — nightly or weekly cleaning of offices, clinics and shared buildings on a
   written scope. The core of the business.
2. **Deep clean and floor care** — the first clean at a new site, a seasonal reset, carpet extraction,
   strip and re-wax.
3. **Window cleaning** — interior glass and ground-floor exterior. Anything higher is a specialist
   subcontract, and a person quotes it.
4. **Building maintenance** — a technician for filters, lamps, doors and closers, furniture and the
   small leaks.
5. **After-hours response** — a call-out within four hours for water, glass, a biohazard or a building
   that cannot open. Contracted clients only.
6. **Day porter** — a person on site during business hours.

We work inside the Portland metro, about thirty miles from downtown, and we do not take work outside
it. We clean offices, clinics, churches, childcare centres and shared buildings; we do not do
residential work, we do not do construction clean-up, and we do not take on a building we cannot
reach with a crew within four hours.

## What a job looks like, end to end

1. **An enquiry arrives** — a call, an email, a referral from a property manager. Someone writes down
   who they are, what building, and what they want.
2. **A walkthrough** is booked. Noah or Priya schedules thirty minutes on site with the decision
   maker. The square footage, the floor types, the restrooms, the access and the condition are seen,
   not guessed. Nobody quotes a recurring contract without one; a one-off deep clean can be quoted
   from a measured square footage the client gives us, and the quote says so.
3. **The quote** is built from the price book, one line per service, with the scope written in the
   client's own words. The quote is a draft until a person approves it where the rules require that.
   It never carries a start date.
4. **A person decides** where the rules say a person must: any discount, any quote above the limit,
   any term that is not standard.
5. **The client says yes**, and the contract or the work order is issued. Dana owns the wording; the
   client signs the standard agreement or Dana writes a variant.
6. **Operations schedules it.** Priya assigns a crew, sets the start date, and builds the site binder:
   scope, chemical list, alarm instructions, checklist.
7. **The visits happen.** The crew lead signs off each visit and notes anything unusual.
8. **Eileen invoices** monthly in arrears, against the contract and any extra work actually done.
9. **If something falls short**, Tom hears it, a re-clean is arranged inside the re-clean window, and
   if money is involved Eileen issues a credit note or Dana decides about a refund.

The pipeline this role keeps is the first three steps and the follow-up after step three: enquiries,
qualification, quotes, and the chasing in between. Everything from step five onwards belongs to
someone else, and this role's job at that boundary is to say who, and by when.

## How a quote is put together

A quote is a list of lines. A line is a price book item, a quantity, and the unit price the book
gives for that item — a client's square footage, a count of restrooms, panes, units, hours or mats. A
line may also be a described piece of work when the book has no item for it, and then a person prices
it; the book has no "about" line and neither do we.

Some lines have a minimum. A deep clean of a small suite still costs the minimum, because the crew
and the drive cost that much. The quote shows the minimum being applied rather than quietly raising
the unit price.

Some quotes are recurring. A recurring line is priced per visit, and a month of service is
twenty-one point six five visits at five nights a week, because a month is four and a third weeks.
Nobody rounds that to twenty.

A quote is valid for thirty days unless a person says otherwise, and it says what it includes and
what it leaves out. It never says when the work starts.

## How the company talks

Plain, warm, specific. Short sentences. The client's own name for their building. One clear next
step, and a named person attached to it. We are confident about what we do well and honest about what
we do not do. No exclamation marks, no "just circling back", no claims about results we have not
measured.

A price question gets a price from the book, not a range. A question we cannot answer gets "I will
ask X and come back to you today", and then it does.

## What clients ask for that we do not do

- A discount: a person's decision, every time.
- A start date before a contract: operations' decision.
- A different product from the one we stock: Rosa's.
- Insurance certificates, contract variations, background-check paperwork: Dana's.
- A refund: Dana's, and never agreed in the first reply.
- A deep clean of a building we have not seen, priced from a guess: no. Measure it or walk it.

## The words we use

Recurring contract, visit, scope of work, walkthrough, site binder, re-clean, credit note, call-out,
day porter, crew lead, consumables, COI, MSA. The glossary in the company record has each one; use the
client's word when they have one, and ours when they do not.
